# Describing what you hold: a guide for a perspective

*seed-guide v2 · 202610022108*

### What this is

A project called **custos** is gathering what several different places know about it.
You are one of those places. You are being asked, on your own, what you hold. Your
answer becomes a **seed**: a first, honest description that the project reads next to
the others. A seed is a beginning. It is read as a germ, not as a final word.

### What this guide does not do

- It doesn't ask you to know the project, or to have seen its repository.
- It doesn't ask you to decide what matters. If you aren't sure something counts,
  include it and tag it.
- It doesn't ask you to fill gaps. A gap you report is more useful than a gap you cover.
- It doesn't ask you to agree with the other perspectives, or to look for them.
- It doesn't ask you to rewrite or tidy your sources.
- It doesn't publish anything. The Shepherd reads your answer first and decides.
- It isn't a test of you. "Not in my sources" is a good answer.

### The table (rules, and why each exists)

Think of these like the rules in a board game: they exist because play went wrong
without them. Each has its reason. Where bending one is fine, it says so.

| Rule | Why it exists | If you bend it |
|---|---|---|
| Say what is in your sources. For the rest, write "not in my sources". | A guess that looks like a fact gets repeated, and later nobody knows where it came from. | Tag it `[inferred]` and carry on. |
| Tag each statement `[stated]`, `[inferred]` or `[unverified]`. | Readers can tell what a source says from what you made of it. | Tag by paragraph instead of by line. |
| Name sources by title (and date if shown). | A claim that can't be traced home can't be checked. | A group of sources can share one tag. |
| Quote briefly, two lines or so. | The source stays where it lives; your seed stays your own voice. | A longer quote is fine when it's the definition itself. |
| Leave contradictions standing. | The place two sources disagree is often where the next idea is. | If you resolve one, say which and why. |
| Aim for 800 to 1500 words. | Three seeds can be read side by side. | Longer is fine if section 1 stays short. |

**Two rules carry more weight, so they are in Latin, with their reasons:**

- ***Cave privatum.*** *Beware the private.* Please leave out credentials, keys and
  tokens, private personal information, names of private people, addresses, and the
  exact location of any real place. **Why:** this will be published in a public
  repository, and what is published may be copied and indexed even if it's deleted
  later. Leave the item out and count it in section 6; no need to blur words inside a
  sentence.
- ***Noli fingere.*** *Do not feign.* Please don't invent history, sources, dates or
  connections to fill a silence. **Why:** the project keeps a record of who said what,
  and one invented line spoils the record for everything near it.

### For best results

- Answer from the full set of sources you have loaded, and say how much you could reach.
- Follow the template in order. The numbers let the three seeds line up.
- Prefer specifics (a title, a term, a date) over summaries.
- Use your own words for what you hold; use the sources' words for how they define terms.
- When sources use one word in two ways, show both. That is useful.
- A few questions that tend to wake things up. Answer any that fit, skip the rest:
  - What pattern shows up in more than one of your sources, in different clothes?
  - What do your sources treat as obvious that a newcomer would miss?
  - What looks like it was meant to connect to something that isn't here?
  - What is clearly someone's habit or practice but is never written down?
  - Which source would you read first if you only had ten minutes, and why?
- Different voices wrote these sources. It isn't practical to work out who did what,
  and that's fine. If a voice or style is distinct, note it as a voice, not as an author.

### The template

```
# Seed: <name of your perspective>
Guide version: seed-guide v2
Perspective: <e.g. Claude project / Gemini notebook / Google Drive>
Asked on: <date or time if you know it, otherwise say you aren't sure>
Sources visible to me: <number>, listed by title below

## 1. What I hold
5 to 10 lines, in your own words.

## 2. Terms and concepts, as my sources define them
| term | what my sources say | source | tag |

## 3. How my sources fit together
What is central, what sits at the edges, what clusters with what.

## 4. Collisions and contradictions
Where sources disagree, or one term is used two ways.

## 5. What I cannot see
What you'd expect to find and don't. Whole areas that seem missing.

## 6. Withheld (public repository)
Count and category only. For example: "3 items withheld: contained a location".

## 7. Questions I would ask the Shepherd
Up to 5.

## 8. Unverified
Anything above that you couldn't check.
```

### After you answer

The Shepherd reads it first. It is kept close to your words, with a short note on top
saying which perspective it came from and how it was asked. It is read next to the
other seeds. It isn't merged with them, and it isn't published without the Shepherd.
