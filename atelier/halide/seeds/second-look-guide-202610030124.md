# A second look: reading the table together

*second-look v1 · 202610030124*

### What this is

Three places that can't see the custos repository were each asked, separately, what they
hold. Their answers were filed and read side by side. This round shows you what the
others reported, and asks you for more: gaps you can fill from your own sources, things
only your seat holds, ideas, and your view of the method. Your answer is read as a germ,
a first honest account, and not as a final word.

### What this guide does not do

- It doesn't ask you to agree with the other seats. Where you hold a different sense of a
  word, that difference is the useful part.
- It doesn't ask you to perform any later step of the process (pouring, scoring,
  publishing). Those need things you can't see.
- It doesn't ask you to know the repository. Where the guide says "the repo holds…", that
  comes from the person who read it, and is tagged `[told]`.
- It doesn't ask for scores or measurements you can't show the inputs for.
- It doesn't publish anything. The Shepherd reads your answer first.
- It isn't a test of you. "Not in my sources" is a good answer.

### The table (rules, and why each exists)

| Rule | Why it exists | If you bend it |
|---|---|---|
| Stay in your seat. Describe what you hold and what you can see from where you sit. | Later steps need things you can't see, and a step taken without them can look finished when it isn't. | If an idea needs a later step, offer it as an idea and say what it would need. |
| Tag each statement: `[stated]` (a named source says it), `[inferred]` (your reading), `[told]` (this guide or the Shepherd said it, not your sources), `[unverified]`. | Readers can tell a source from a reading from a message. | Tag by paragraph. |
| Open with your reach: how many sources you saw, how many you opened or read. | It is the single most useful line for judging the rest. | A rough count is fine. |
| Show the inputs behind a number, or leave the number out. | A figure with no inputs reads like a measurement and can't be checked. | An illustration is fine if it says it is one. |
| Leave contradictions standing. | The seam between two sources is often where the next idea is. | If you resolve one, say which and why. |
| Aim for roughly 800 to 1500 words, and put long lists in an appendix. | The answers need to be read side by side. | Longer is fine if section 1 stays short. |

**Two rules carry more weight, so they are in Latin, with their reasons:**

- ***Cave privatum.*** *Beware the private.* Please leave out credentials, keys and tokens;
  private personal information; names or handles of private people; addresses; folder IDs
  and similar identifiers; and **any real place that could be found on a map, including
  ZIP or postal codes, named rivers, confluences, watersheds and corners.** **Why:** this
  will be published in a public repository, and published text may be copied and indexed
  even if it's deleted later. Leave the item out and count it in section 7. A seat that
  reports "withheld: 0" will be read by someone who checks.
- ***Noli fingere.*** *Do not feign.* Please don't invent sources, dates, quotes, links or
  figures to fill a silence. **Why:** the project keeps a record of who said what, and one
  invented line spoils the lines next to it.

### How to take the second look

Pick the parts that fit and skip the rest. Your appendix has leads that came from the
other seats' answers.

1. **Fill.** Answer, from your sources, the questions in your appendix. Quote briefly, name
   the source, tag each answer.
2. **Add.** Say what only your seat can see, which no one else's seed has: a title, a
   term, a structure, a habit that is never written down.
3. **Offer ideas.** Up to five. Each says what would be needed to try it. Some prompts that
   tend to help:
   - *What does a gap you found want to be called?*
   - *What resembles something in another language of the world (code, music notation,
     weather, tides, a loom, a bee's dance, morse) in your sources?* Tag it `[inferred]`.
   - *What is one small experiment that could run this week, and how would we know it
     worked?*
   - *What would you add if no one were checking?*
   - *Which rule in this guide would you change, and why?*
4. **React to the valuation.** The repo scores documents with five questions: a clear title
   (+2), a datable stamp (+2), complete rather than fragmentary content (+3), connects to
   other system documents (+2), assigned a destination (+1). Ten is the top. Your seed
   scored what your appendix says. Does that fit? Then pick three documents from your own
   sources, score them the same way, and say where the five questions struggled.
5. **Say how the method went.** What was clear? Where did you hesitate? Which words in the
   first guide pulled you toward something you weren't asked? Did you add the guide to
   your sources? What would make this easier to answer, and what would you take out?
