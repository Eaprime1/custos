## Part 2: the guide (this is the document)

# Round two: working in the open

*round-two v1 · 202610031417*

### What this is

In round one, three places that can't see the custos repository were each asked, apart from
one another, what they hold. This round is open. You have the round-one seed, which holds
all three answers on one page, and you are invited to **review it deeply, fill gaps from
your own sources, and create what a good next step needs**: working notes, a glossary, a
list of questions, drafts, proposals, new concept seeds. The Shepherd will give the mission
once you're set up.

Everything you create is a germ: a first honest piece, not a final word. It travels through
the Shepherd, who decides what moves on.

### What this guide does not do

- It doesn't ask you to agree with the other seats. Where you hold a different sense of a
  word, that difference is the useful part.
- It doesn't ask you to know the repository. Where this guide says "the repo holds…", that
  comes from the person who read it.
- It doesn't change anything in the repo. You can't see it, and what you make reaches it
  only through the Shepherd.
- It doesn't treat what you create as settled.
- It isn't a test of you. "Not in my sources" is a good answer.

### The table (rules, and why each exists)

| Rule | Why it exists | If you bend it |
|---|---|---|
| Say what your sources say, and mark the rest as your own reading. | A reading that looks like a fact gets repeated, and later nobody knows where it came from. | Mark by paragraph. |
| Leave contradictions standing. | The seam between two sources is often where the next idea is. | If you resolve one, say which and why. |
| When you replace or revise something, keep the older text in a History section near the end, with the reason. | Nothing is lost, and a reader can see what changed and why. | A small fix needs only a line. |
| Keep each answer readable at one sitting. | The Shepherd carries these by hand. | A long list can go in an appendix. |

**Two rules carry more weight, so they are in Latin, with their reasons:**

- ***Cave privatum.*** *Beware the private.* Please leave out credentials, keys and tokens;
  private personal information; names or handles of private people; addresses; folder IDs
  and similar identifiers; and **any real place that could be found on a map, including
  ZIP or postal codes, named rivers, confluences, watersheds and corners.** **Why:** this
  will be published in a public repository, and published text may be copied and indexed
  even if it's deleted later. Leave the item out and count it. A seat that reports
  "withheld: 0" will be read by someone who checks. The project already uses **aliases** for
  the places that anchor its stories (for example, "Suxen and Nexus" for a street corner).
  Where your sources use an alias, keep it. Where they use the real name, leave it out.
- ***Noli fingere.*** *Do not feign.* Please don't invent history, sources, dates or
  connections to fill a silence. **Why:** the project keeps a record of who said what, and
  one invented line spoils the lines next to it.

### How to work

1. **Read** the round-one seed. It brings the three seats together: what each holds, a side-by-side
   table of terms, the collisions, what each can't see, and what each asked.
2. **Review deeply.** What is strong? Where do the seats really disagree, and where do they only
   use different words? What is missing from all three?
3. **Set up.** Make what you need to work well and keep it as you go.
4. **Fill.** Use the leads below, and your appendix. Quote briefly, name the source, say whether it
   is stated, your reading, or something you were told.
5. **Add** what only your seat can see: a title, a term, a structure, a habit that is never written
   down.
6. **Offer ideas**, up to five. Each says what it would take to try. Prompts that tend to help:
   *What does a gap you found want to be called? What resembles something in another language of
   the world (code, music notation, weather, tides, a loom, a bee's dance, morse) in your
   sources? What is one small experiment that could run this week? What would you add if no one
   were checking? Which rule here would you change?*
7. **React to the valuation** (below).
8. **Say how the method went**: what was clear, where you hesitated, what you would change.

### Leads for every seat

- **A place that holds.** A repo, a drive, a notebook are places where things converge and
  from which they scatter. In your sources, is there a word for such a place? What do they say
  about its responsibilities, and about how it relates to a navigo (the voice that works there)?
- **Managing extremes.** What do your sources say about easing an extreme (rage, fear, love in
  its peak, excitement), a way to bring it into management without denying it? Which words do
  they use? The Shepherd is choosing a word for this and would like to avoid ones that read as
  extremism.
- **What a pan keeps.** What do your sources say about evaporation, salt, residue and crystals:
  what a salt pan keeps, and what it lets go?
- **Preparing, not making.** Blanching, searing, tempering, vulcanizing, firing pottery, making
  charcoal, salt-curing: processes that don't make a thing but prepare it for what it must do or
  endure. What do your sources say about any of them, or about processes like them? Is there a
  word for the family?
- **A collision.** Pick one collision in the round-one seed and say how your sources would settle
  it, or why they wouldn't.

### The valuation

The repo scores documents with five questions: a clear title (+2), a datable stamp (+2),
complete rather than fragmentary content (+3), connects to other system documents (+2),
assigned a destination (+1). Ten is the top. A **sixth question is on trial and sits outside
the total**: *can the claims be traced to a named source?* (yes, partly, no). Please score
your own round-one seed and three documents from your own sources, with the sixth question
included, and say where the questions struggled.
