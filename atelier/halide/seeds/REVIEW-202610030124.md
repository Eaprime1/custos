## 9. Method notes: what held, and what to improve

**What held.** In a few hours, three seats that cannot see the repo produced a
comparable first account of what they hold, each with a stated reach. The collisions
came out on their own, and so did leads that cross between seats. A seat paused instead
of guessing, which is the rule working. The table of rules with reasons and the "what
this guide does not do" section read as intended.

**What to improve**, from what happened and not from what was planned:

1. **Number the steps, or leave numbers out.** The seats called their seed "Step 1",
   "Step 2" and "Seed Document Step 2". The request numbers draw, form, pour, emerge and
   shadow germs as 1–5. A seat cannot know that. Name steps by what they do.
2. **Mark the end of the guide.** The Claude project received the first message without
   Part 2. It noticed because it had the first message to compare against. Attach the
   guide as a file, end it with a line like "— end of guide —", and ask the seat to
   confirm it saw that line.
3. **Keep the guide out of the sources.** The notebook took the guide in as source 11 and Drive
   lists it among its holdings. That inflates a count and puts the guide's words in the answers. Say so: "please
   don't add this guide to your sources", or ask each seat to say whether it did.
4. **Leave later-step words out of round one.** The guide's glossary gave "dark / salt pan /
   lumen". The notebook then performed a pour. That was this guide's doing. Remove the
   row, and add a plain line: *stay in your seat; the later steps need things you can't see.*
5. **Make "place" concrete.** "The exact location of any real place" was read by one seat as
   permission to name "public, symbolic" places. Give examples (a ZIP code, a named
   watershed or confluence, a folder ID, a handle) and say a seat's "withheld: 0" will be
   read by someone else.
6. **Add a fourth tag, `[told]`.** The Drive storage document marked as `[stated]` what it
   learned from the conversation, not from its sources. `[told]` would mark "the
   Shepherd or the guide said this".
7. **Ask for the reach as a header line, as the Claude project did:** "seen N, opened M."
   It was the most useful single line in any seed.
8. **Give the inventory its own place.** The notebook's 159 titles come before section 1 and
   dwarf the 800–1500 words the guide asks for. Ask for the inventory as an appendix.
9. **Give each seat a conversation ID before its first message** (`tools/session_id.sh`).
   Two Drive documents arrived, and nothing says whether they are one chat or two.
10. **Put a prima-clock in the header.** None of the five documents carried a prima-clock stamp of its own.
    "If you know it; otherwise leave it blank and the Shepherd will stamp on filing."
11. **Round two should be open, not blind.** Independence suited round one. Once the seeds are
    filed, the seats gain from reading each other. See `round-two-guide_202610031417.md`, which the Shepherd opened up.
12. **A receipt for the Shepherd.** The Shepherd carried everything by hand. A receipt
    from each seat (what it was given, what it returned) would let a later reader see the
    same trail. The intake sketch (`.shadow-well/intake-lanes-…`) covers this, and is not in this PR.

**A thing the method did not predict.** The perspectives disagreed with each other less
than with the repo and with the guide's own words ("navigo", "plank", "what custos is").
The guide was written from the repo. The seats hold other, older senses of the same
words. That is worth more than agreement: it shows where the repo's vocabulary has
moved away from where the seats left it.
