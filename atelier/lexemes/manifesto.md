## What the source says

A manifesto is a public declaration of intentions, motives, or views issued by an individual, group, or government. The word derives from Latin *manifestus* — made public, evident, plain. Historically it carries an absolute, declarative quality: it proclaims what *is* and what *will be*, often as a founding act of a movement. Famous examples (Communist Manifesto, Futurist Manifesto, Cluetrain Manifesto) tend to be sweeping, unconditional, and written as if the declared position is settled truth.

## Why it is distressed here

The absolute register of the word carries weight. A manifesto *declares*; it does not propose, invite, or explore. In environments where contributors are doing genuine discovery work — where a statement could foreclose a still-open question — the lexeme can act as a door that closes rather than opens. The word also carries significant trauma associations in some contexts: political declarations of that form have been used to exclude, erase, and harm.

The specific phrase "defining individual authorship is impossible" in the Voices of Navigo draft is an example of the manifesto register bleeding into the document's content. That claim may be true in some contexts; it is not settled here and should not land as a founding axiom before the question has actually been worked.

## What it could do in custos

**When the word is necessary:** If a document truly *is* a public declaration with unconditional authority behind it — a finalized charter, a policy with binding force — the word may be correct. Invoke it deliberately and note why the absolute register is appropriate.

**Substitute candidates:** *charter*, *framework*, *declaration* (softer), *principles*, *accord*, *position paper*, *proposal*. The choice depends on how settled the content actually is.

**Satire / idle-adventurer angle (seed):** The absolute quality of the lexeme makes it a good target for a satirical or idle-adventurer character whose role is to *search* for absolute lexemes, issue a runic-coded flag at the location, and create a report — without repeating the word beyond what is necessary. The character could operate on a scan-and-flag loop, writing findings in runic shorthand so the word is not reproduced in plain text any more than needed. This is a seed, not a design.
