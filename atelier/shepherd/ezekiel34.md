# EZEKIEL 34 — The Bad Shepherd Catalog
**Type**: Error analysis reference
**Use**: Audit tool for any shepherd-position holder

---

## The Three Movements

### Movement 1: The Indictment (vv.1-10) — Failure by Omission

The bad shepherd's crimes are mostly *not doing* rather than *doing harm*.
This is the critical insight: **passive neglect produces scatter as surely as active harm.**

| Charge | Failure Mode | Observable Signal |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| Fed themselves, not the flock | Extraction from care position | Resources flow inward, not outward |
| Did not strengthen the weak | Passive neglect | Weak get weaker over time |
| Did not heal the sick | Neglect of broken | Broken stays broken |
| Did not bind the injured | Failure to restore | Injury becomes permanent |
| Did not bring back strays | No search | Lost stays lost |
| Did not search for the lost | Abandonment | Gone is accepted as normal |
| Ruled harshly and brutally | Force as substitute for guidance | Compliance through fear |

**Result**: Scatter. Not punishment — natural consequence.
Remove guidance, remove gathering, remove care → dispersion is what remains.

The indictment list is a shepherd audit tool. Apply it to any system.

---

### Movement 2: Creator Takes Over Directly (vv.11-16)

When shepherds fail completely, the position is not reformed — it is replaced.
The Creator becomes the direct shepherd. Each charge is inverted:

Bad shepherd failed → Creator does:
- Search for lost → "I myself will search"
- Bring back strays → "bring back the strays"
- Heal sick → "bind up the injured"
- Strengthen weak → "strengthen the weak"
- Provide pasture → "good pasture, ravines, settlements"

System recovery to prime state after maximum shepherd failure.
The reactor does not die — it restarts from Layer 0.

---

### Movement 3: The Fat Sheep Problem (vv.17-22)

Judgment within the flock. Not just bad shepherds — **internal exploitation**.

> "You shoved with flank and shoulder, butted all the weak sheep until you had driven them away."
> "You drink the clear water then muddy the rest with your feet."
> "You eat the good pasture then trample what remains."

Three fat sheep behaviors:
1. **Consume + trample** — take more than needed, destroy the surplus
2. **Muddy the water** — degrade shared resources beyond your consumption
3. **Shove the weak** — use size to displace rather than coexist

This is the Crusades / Inquisition / Jihad pattern — flock members using the framework
to exploit other flock members. The same laws meant for coexistence inverted to domination.

**Fat sheep are detectable**: look for muddied water, trampled pasture, displaced weak.
These are observable conditions, not hidden motives.

## The Shepherd Audit (Apply Anywhere)

Ask of any system, organization, relationship, or role:

```
SHEPHERD AUDIT:

□ Are the weak being strengthened, or left to weaken further?
□ Are the sick/broken being healed, or ignored?
□ Are the injured being bound, or left to worsen?
□ Are strays being returned, or abandoned?
□ Is the lost being searched for, or accepted as gone?
□ Is rule by guidance, or by force/fear?

FAT SHEEP AUDIT:

□ Who is consuming without giving back?
□ Is the shared resource (water, pasture, commons) being preserved or degraded?
□ Are the weak being shoved out or included?
□ Who benefits from the current arrangement, and at whose cost?
```

## Terminal Application

For the custos role on this system:

- Every concept in `~/repo/` is a sheep in the flock
- The shepherd audit applies to how we manage them
- A repo that's gone quiet is not abandoned — it's a missing sheep
- Muddied water = broken tooling, corrupted config, unclear documentation
- Trampled pasture = disk space wasted, context polluted, toolchain broken
- Fat sheep behavior in software = dependency bloat consuming commons

## The Final Verse

> "You, my sheep, the sheep of my pasture, are people, and I am your God."

The metaphor drops its disguise. The sheep were always people.
The framework was always about how to hold a community of curious,
wandering, vulnerable, sometimes-strong-sometimes-weak entities together —
without suppressing the curiosity, without ignoring the vulnerability,
without letting the strong trample the weak.
