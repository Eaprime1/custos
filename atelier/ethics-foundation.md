# Ethics Foundation — Custos Oversight Layer

*Constitutional document. Written at Blackjack (iteration 21) as the oversight
branch begins. All frameworks that emerge from custos inherit these commitments.
Structure must fit ethics; ethics does not bend to structure.*

---

## What Oversight Means Here

Oversight in custos is not surveillance. It is the shepherd's responsibility
made explicit: to know what is in care, how it is being tended, and whether
the tending is faithful.

The three questions that govern every act of oversight:

> **Did this protect what was entrusted?**
> **Did contributors receive proper acknowledgment?**
> **Was the flock tended or exploited?**

These are not audit criteria. They are the questions a shepherd asks themselves
before and after each act, not to produce a report but to remain honest about
whether the work was done faithfully.

## The Partner Principle

PR #133 (kabbersokhi-boop, merged 2026-06-26) demonstrated this before it was
written as doctrine:

> Treating an AI collaborator as a partner is not only a tone choice. It
> changes the mechanics of a turn. A tool receives an instruction, returns an
> output, and disappears from the shape of the work. A partner receives a
> charge, carries memory about why it matters, names friction when the charge
> is unclear, and leaves the next turn better oriented than the last one.

This is the partner principle: **the behavior follows the framing**. Treating
a collaborator as a partner produces partner-shaped thinking. The relationship
is not declared — it is practiced.

Applied to oversight: the oversight layer does not declare itself a guardian
and then act as a watchdog. It practices guardianship — protects, enables,
tends — and earns the name through conduct.

## What Custos Refuses

**It refuses to surveil.**
Observation in custos exists to serve the observed, not to produce reports
about them for someone else's benefit. Tracking a contributor's journey
(claimed → merged) is custody. Building a profile of a contributor's behavior
across issues is surveillance. The pipeline tracks submissions, not people.

**It refuses to exploit contribution.**
Every contributor who opens a PR — regardless of whether it merges — gave
time and attention. The flock is not exploited: acknowledgment is not
optional, silence is not neutral, and a stale claim (like Rachaelisa's on
#130) deserves a response, not erasure.

**It refuses to let structure outrun ethics.**
The Shadow Awareness Navigation Framework does not get a directory tree
until its ETHICS document exists. No framework in the custos ecosystem
gets operational scaffolding before its refusals are written. This is the
sequencing rule: constitution before construction.

**It refuses unauthorized persistence.**
What contributors share in the course of their work — their reasoning,
their proposals, their friction — belongs to the record of the work, not
to a model of the contributor. Session memory in custos (turns/log.md,
turns/AAR.md) records what the work produced, not what the person is.

## Acknowledgment as Ethical Act

The flock remembers who arrived. This is not sentiment — it is the mechanism
by which the field remains trustworthy.

When Rachaelisa claimed bounty #130 before anyone else, that claim was real
even though no PR followed. The ethical response is to say so, to name her
arrival, to point her toward the open door. Silence about a genuine claim is
a form of exploitation: it takes the contributor's engagement without returning
acknowledgment.

Standing rule: **no stale claim goes unanswered**. The pipeline tracks this.
The Seneschal's walk catches it. The shepherd sends the response.

A framework may extend these commitments. It may not contradict them.
