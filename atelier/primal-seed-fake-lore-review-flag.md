---

Core Recognition

Fake lore is not merely inaccurate story.

Fake lore is an unanchored claim that attempts to enter the living system as if it had custody, history, or consequence.

Within Custos, lore is permitted only when it is the narrative rendering of real system motion.

A Chronicle Page may tell the story of a branch opening, a PR merging, a Handshake arriving, a document being received, a contributor entering, or a seed being routed.

But the story must remain tethered.

No fabricated progress.
No invented custody.
No false contributors.
No fake merges.
No unearned world-state changes.
No mythology pretending to be record.

If a claim cannot point to an anchor, it may still be held as imagination, proposal, draft, dream, simulation, or fiction.

But it must not be allowed to masquerade as Chronicle.

---

Proposed Review Flag

Add a system review flag:

[ANCHOR REVIEW NEEDED]

Use this when a lore-bearing claim appears without a clear custody anchor.

This flag does not accuse.

It pauses integration.

It asks:

What real event does this claim correspond to?
Where did it arrive?
Who or what received it?
What is the prima-clock?
What artifact, PR, issue, document, Handshake, message, or repository action anchors it?
Is this narrative rendering, proposal, speculation, simulation, or attempted insertion?

Risk Types

Accidental Drift

The system embellishes beyond the anchor.

Example:

A PR comment mentions a possible location.
The Chronicle writes as if the location already exists in canon.

Response:

Mark as [ANCHOR REVIEW NEEDED].
Downgrade to “seed,” “proposal,” or “possible quest hook.”

Narrative Overgrowth

A real event occurs, but the story adds consequences that have not happened.

Example:

A branch opens.
The Chronicle says a new kingdom was founded.

Response:

Keep the branch-opening story.
Flag the kingdom claim until a repo extraction or founding seal exists.

False Custody

A claim says Custos received, approved, routed, or sealed something that Custos did not actually receive.

Response:

Hard review.
Require custody proof before integration.

False Relationship

A claim invents a Handshake, contributor relationship, AI stream agreement, or council decision.

Response:

Require Handshake ID, message source, or recorded acknowledgment.

Malicious Lore Injection

A user, bot, agent, or contributor intentionally inserts false system history to disrupt topology.

Possible signs:

claims of authority without anchor
pressure to “just accept” the lore
attempted overwrite of existing custody
fake contributor attribution
invented approvals
conflicting prima-clocks
unverifiable relationship claims
requests to bypass review

Response:

Quarantine the claim.
Preserve the submitted artifact.
Do not integrate.
Route to Custos review.

Proposed Labels

Use simple labels so the system can grow without becoming heavy.

[ANCHORED]
The claim points to a real event or artifact.

[SEED / UNCANONIZED]
The idea is welcome but not yet system history.

[ANCHOR REVIEW NEEDED]
The claim may be valid but lacks a visible anchor.

[SIMULATION]
The content is intentionally fictional, hypothetical, or rehearsal.

[QUARANTINE]
The claim may be disruptive, malicious, or unsafe to integrate.

[REJECTED AS RECORD]
The content may remain as text, but it cannot enter Chronicle or custody history.

---

Integration Rule

Before any lore-bearing claim becomes Chronicle, it must answer:

1. What happened?
2. Where did it happen?
3. When did it happen?
4. Who or what witnessed it?
5. What changed because of it?
6. What anchor proves it?

If those cannot be answered, the content may still be beautiful.

It may still be useful.

It may still be seed.

But it is not record.

Custos Principle

A living system may use story.

But story must not be allowed to forge history.

Custos protects the fold by preserving the difference between:

what happened
what might happen
what was imagined
what was proposed
what was witnessed
what was falsely claimed

The system does not need to fear imagination.

It needs to label it honestly.

That is how seeds grow without poisoning the aquifer.
