# COMMISSION: LUKE 15 — THE DEPTH CHARGE
**Status**: Queued — foundational framework extension
**Context**: Anti-optimization principle. The one and the ninety-nine.

---

## The Depth Charge Eric Named

The rejoicing when the one is found is described as *greater* than the rejoicing
over the ninety-nine that never wandered.

This breaks standard value accounting entirely.
One < ninety-nine in any optimization model.
One > ninety-nine in this framework.

The asymmetry only resolves from *relationship*, not mathematics.

---

## The Translation Problem (Already Identified)

Greek: *kataleipō* — to leave behind, to leave in a place
English: "leave" — implies abandonment

A responsible shepherd leaves the 99 in custody:
- With another shepherd
- With trained guard dogs
- In a secure sheepfold with a gatekeeper
- With a hired hand (sufficient for holding, not for wolf defense)

The story is not about abandoning the 99.
The story is about the shepherd and the lost.

## Research Questions

### 1. The Original Language

- Full Greek text of Luke 15:3-7
- What is the actual word translated "leave"?
- What is the word translated "lost"? (*apollumi* — to destroy, lose, perish — stronger than "misplaced")
- What is the word for "found"? (*heuriskō* — to find, but also to discover, to come upon)
- What does the word for "rejoicing" actually mean? Is it the same word used for the 99?

### 2. The Rejoicing Asymmetry

> "I tell you that in the same way there will be more rejoicing in heaven over one sinner
> who repents than over ninety-nine righteous persons who do not need to repent."

- Why is the rejoicing *greater* for the one?
- What does the rejoicing of the 99 look like? Is it described?
- The 99 who "do not need to repent" — are they actually fine, or just not lost yet?
- Is this a statement about the value of the one, or about the nature of the shepherd?

### 3. The Three Parables of Luke 15

Luke 15 contains three lost-and-found parables:
- Lost sheep (man/shepherd)
- Lost coin (woman)
- Lost son (prodigal) — the longest, the most famous

Are they three versions of the same framework?
What does each add that the others don't?
The lost son chooses to leave — different from a sheep wandering.
What's the triage difference?

### 4. The "Why Lost" Question

Eric identified the two failure modes:
- Wandered (curiosity, self-directed)
- Predator/injury (external force)

Is there a third? The prodigal son leaves *by choice*.
This is neither wandering nor external force.
How does the shepherd respond differently to voluntary departure?

### 5. Historical Shepherd Practice

Would an actual shepherd in 1st century Judea/Galilee leave the flock to find one?
What is the actual practice?
- Multiple shepherd cooperation (relevant — see John 10 multi-herd pen)
- Guard dog use (common in the region)
- Sheepfold structure and security
- What would the actual risk calculation be?
