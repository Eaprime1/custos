# Community Gardening Initiative

**Source:** Navigo14, "Doable First" companion to Feral Gardens for Wildlife Habitat

One standout idea from the brainstorming ideas: **creating a community gardening initiative** (Feral Gardens). Actionable, and can foster community engagement as well as promote local food sources.

## Doable First Action: Community Gardening Initiative

### Step 1: Define the Concept

- **Objective**: Establish a community garden that allows residents to plant and cultivate fruits, vegetables, and herbs, promoting sustainability and local food production.
- **Type**: Feral gardens where plants grow naturally, requiring minimal maintenance.

### Step 2: Identify Resources

- **Location**: Find a suitable piece of land (public parks, vacant lots) that can be used for the garden.
- **Community Involvement**: Engage local residents, schools, and organizations to participate and contribute.
- **Funding**: Explore local grants, sponsorships, or crowdfunding options to support the initiative.

### Step 3: Create a Plan

- **Timeline**: Set a timeline for garden setup, planting, and maintenance.
- **Roles**: Define roles for volunteers (garden coordinators, maintenance crew, educators).
- **Workshops**: Plan workshops on gardening techniques, sustainability practices, and cooking with garden produce.

### Step 4: Launch the Initiative

- **Promotion**: Use social media, local newspapers, and community boards to promote the garden and recruit volunteers.
- **Kick-off Event**: Organize a launch event to gather the community, provide information, and start planting.

### Step 5: Monitor and Evaluate

- **Feedback Loop**: Create a system for community feedback to improve the garden experience.
- **Sustainability**: Develop a plan for ongoing maintenance and expansion of the garden.

## Next Steps

1. **Research Local Regulations**: Check for any local regulations regarding community gardens.
2. **Engage Stakeholders**: Start reaching out to potential community members and organizations for support and involvement.
3. **Set Up a Meeting**: Organize an initial meeting to gauge interest and gather ideas from the community.

This community gardening initiative not only aligns with the goals of fostering sustainability and community engagement but is also a tangible project that can be started immediately.
