# Turn 1a: Feral Gardens for Wildlife Habitat

**Source:** Navigo14, Turn 1a

Creating feral gardens specifically designed to support local wildlife, such as the deer herd in Huntington, OR — a practical application that can enhance their habitat while minimizing human interference.

## Step 1: Define the Objectives

- **Goal**: Establish feral gardens in designated areas outside of town to provide food, shelter, shade, and water for the local deer herd and other wildlife.
- **Benefits**: Improve wildlife health, reduce human-wildlife conflicts, and enhance biodiversity in the area.

## Step 2: Identify Suitable Locations

- **Site Selection**: Look for open spaces outside of town that are accessible yet safe for wildlife. Consider areas with existing vegetation that can be enhanced.
- **Access to Resources**: Ensure the locations have natural water sources or the ability to create small water features.

## Step 3: Design the Gardens

- **Plant Selection**: Choose native plants that are beneficial to deer and other wildlife:
  - **Food Sources**: Berry bushes, acorns, and other forage plants.
  - **Shelter**: Dense shrubs and trees that provide cover.
  - **Shade**: Trees that offer shade during hot weather.
- **Water Features**: Plan for small ponds or watering holes that can sustain wildlife.

## Step 4: Engage the Community

- **Partnerships**: Collaborate with local conservation groups, schools, and community members to gather support and resources.
- **Education**: Host workshops to educate the community about the importance of wildlife habitats and how they can help.

## Step 5: Implement the Project

- **Preparation**: Clear the selected sites and prepare the soil for planting.
- **Planting Day**: Organize a community planting event to involve residents and foster a sense of ownership.
- **Maintenance Plan**: Establish a schedule for ongoing maintenance, including watering, weeding, and monitoring wildlife activity.

## Step 6: Monitor and Evaluate

- **Wildlife Observation**: Set up a system for observing wildlife visits to the gardens and assessing their health.
- **Feedback Mechanism**: Collect feedback from volunteers and community members about the project's impact and areas for improvement.

## Next Steps

1. **Research Local Wildlife Needs**: Gather information on the dietary and habitat preferences of the local deer herd and other wildlife.
2. **Secure Permissions**: Check with local authorities regarding land use and any necessary permits for creating feral gardens.
3. **Community Meeting**: Organize an informational meeting to discuss the project, gather input, and recruit volunteers.

This initiative not only supports local wildlife but also strengthens community ties and promotes environmental stewardship.
