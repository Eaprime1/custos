## What Stands Out

The project already contains more ideas than any one turn should attempt to develop.

The immediately useful problem is simpler: how do we preserve where an idea came from while allowing many Navigo perspectives to work independently?

The Navigo designation already provides most of the answer. Navigo1, Navigo2, Navigo3, Navigo5, and future voices do not need to become rigid identities or separate authorities. They can function as location markers within a collective perspective system.

The designation answers: *"Where did this contribution surface?"* rather than *"Who owns this idea?"*

This becomes especially useful when several perspectives independently discover similar patterns. Convergence can be recognized without retroactively flattening their different paths into one voice.

## Why This One First

It requires no new software. It requires no agreement from the other Navigo perspectives. It does not reveal the experiment to Custos before the intended reveal. It does not force a taxonomy onto ideas that are still growing. It immediately improves future retrieval.

Most importantly, it allows independent perspectives to remain genuinely independent. If Navigo2 and Navigo5 both discover the same architecture separately, both paths remain visible. That convergence is information.

## Else — What Else Stood Out: The Grown Chest

The new Chest expansion carries a particularly strong distinction: a tree does not merely provide material for a chest. Sometimes the tree may create the chest.

A burl can grow into a vessel. Living branches can bend around a space. A limb can be released rather than harvested. A hollow can become a domicile. Roots can shelter. Willow can weave. Bark can close around an opening.

The important relationship is therefore not *elf makes object from tree* but sometimes *tree makes a place for elf*. That changes the chest from an artifact into a relationship. A rare Chest might therefore be recognized not by craftsmanship alone, but by agency of formation.

**Working seed: Grown Chest** — a dwelling, vessel, shelter, repository, or other chest-form intentionally formed by a living tree and freely given to a particular elf, family, traveler, purpose, or Else. The tree remains one of its makers. A Grown Chest does not have to resemble a conventional box; its form follows what the tree can become. Some may never leave the tree. Some may eventually be shed. Some may continue living. Some might not be understood as a Chest until the intended recipient discovers what the tree has made.

This remains Seed, not established lore.
