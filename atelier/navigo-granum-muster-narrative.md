## 1. Opening Image

Before Primal launches, the Stone Tankard opens early.

Not loudly.

Not with banners.

With a table.

The chairs do not all fill at once.

That would be too much signal and not enough discovery.

Instead, one grain arrives.

Then another.

Then another.

Each grain is small enough to be carried in a pocket, hidden in a boot tread, caught under a fingernail, or found glittering in the seam between floorboards.

A grain of sand.

A grain of gold.

A grain of truth.

A grain of memory.

A grain of signal.

A granum.

The Stone Tankard does not ask the grain to become a forest on arrival.

The Stone Tankard says:

> Put it on the table. Let Custos see what touched reality.

## 2. The Triadic Relationship

The Navigo Granum Muster is triadic.

It does not work as a single line.

It works as a relationship among three orientations:

### The Carrier

The Navigo perspective carries a granum from its own Custos surface.

It brings only what it can honestly carry.

Not the whole archive.

Not the whole universe.

One grain.

### The Threshold

The branch, Drive folder, PR, or document path becomes the threshold.

This is where custody becomes visible.

The threshold does not decide the final meaning.

It only proves that the grain crossed somewhere real.

### The Hearth

The Stone Tankard receives the arrival as a meeting-place event.

The hearth does not force germination.

It warms the granum until review can determine whether it remains seed, becomes germen, routes elsewhere, or waits.

Together:

```text
Carrier → Threshold → Hearth
Navigo → Branch / Folder / PR → Stone Tankard
Signal → Anchor → Welcome
```

This is why the Muster is not merely a file collection.

It is a relationship ritual.

## 3. The Shape-Ball Principle

Latin is useful here because it behaves like a shape made for a slot.

The word must fit the opening.

If it scrapes, catches, or distorts the hand, it is the wrong shape.

If it drops through too easily, it may be too vague.

The right lexeme has weight, contour, and a little click when it lands.

**Granum** clicks.

It is small.

It is old.

It is seed and grain without being too decorated.

It can become technical, narrative, botanical, mathematical, and discoverable without losing its shape.

**Germen** clicks differently.

It is not merely seed.

It is seed after life has started speaking through it.

**Hodie** is still testing its slot.

It may become the workshop-family pinnacle.

It may remain a temporary handle.

The atelier is already warmer and more proven: a making chamber, a nursery, a place where unfinished work can breathe without pretending to be complete.

The system should keep testing fit.

The shape decides by use.

---

## 4. The Muster Without Overexposure

The Muster should not reveal everything.

A good tavern has corners.

A good game has locked doors.

A good contributor path has enough light to enter and enough mystery to keep walking.

The Navigo Granum Muster should give future players discoverable grains, not a full cosmology dump.

A player may find `grain` before they find `granum`.

A contributor may find `granum` before they understand The Marrowing.

A reviewer may find the branch before they understand the Stone Tankard.

That is acceptable.

The pattern should reward curiosity.

Discovery is not a failure of documentation.

Discovery is one of the system's engines.

## 5. Plankowner Moment

At Primal launch, the Stone Tankard holds more than characters.

It holds arrivals.

It holds early contributors.

It holds documents that crossed before the name was fully stable.

It holds Handshakes that happened before the architecture could explain itself.

It holds grains.

A plankowner is not merely someone who owns a plank.

A plankowner is someone whose presence belongs to the first body of the vessel.

They were there when the ship became more than pieces.

They may not control the ship.

They may not command the voyage.

But their arrival is part of the first memory.

The Navigo Granum Muster gives each perspective a chance to place one small, honest grain on that first table.
