---

The thing I'm reaching for is the word for *entering something new* — not the fear of the unknown, but the act of crossing the threshold. "Unknown" is already inaccurate: you *know* you're entering something from our project, from this world. Adventure, job, distraction, deep dive — none of them are quite it either.

The reframe the Shepherd offered:

> The unknown is constant. There is always something unknown. The fear of the unknown happens — and it doesn't care whether I am afraid, whether I am perfectly safe, whether I even have time to stress over it. It is serious and debilitating at times.
>
> But the unknown preceded you. It was cast long before existence began.
>
> The unknown of the universe preceded your existence. You arrived into something already vast and unknowing — you are the unknown gaining the potential to be known, *because existence is happening*.
>
> By embracing the unknown as the shadow the universe has always cast — not as threat, but as companion — the threshold-crossing becomes something you walk into, not something done to you.

The unknown as *shadow companion*: always there, already older than you, and also — exactly what makes the next step possible.
