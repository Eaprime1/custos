## The what

Two claims from the notes, stated so they can be tested:

- **H1, inverse relation.** For a given subject, the more an observer knows about it, the lower the complexity
  the observer reports.
- **H2, three-perspective scoring.** Rating a subject through three groups of measures (physical, mental, essential),
  each normalized to 0-1 and averaged, gives steadier scores than one single rating.

The epic exists to find out whether these hold. If complexity rises with knowledge for some subjects, that is a finding,
not a failure.

## Fixes needed before any field work (proposal)

- **Define both axes.** Knowledge = score on a short quiz about the subject. Complexity = the observer's rating (1 to 10)
  plus a count of the distinct components they list.
- **Normalize both to 0-1.** The crossing point in the notes (where the two curves meet) means nothing in raw units.
  Once both are normalized it becomes a measurable "familiarity point" that can be compared across subjects.
- **Time** means the number of exposures or sessions with the subject, not clock time.

## How other conversations take part

Any conversation, on any platform, can contribute by returning one **Contribution Packet**. The paste-in text is in
`packet-template.md`. Returned packets go in `packets/`, and each one gets a line in `LOG.md`. The creator is always
recorded.

## Cautions, carried forward

- **No people as subjects.** Subjects are things (an object, a recipe, a rule, a concept). No personal or identifying
  data about real people goes into packets. The Shepherd's private notes are not source material for the survey.
- **Consent.** Anything involving human respondents is voluntary, explained in plain language, and can be withdrawn.
- **No cash.** custos pays no monetary rewards. This seed promises no reward of any kind. How contributions are
  recognized is for the Shepherd to decide.
- **Do not assume the answer.** Do not write a packet to make H1 come out true.
