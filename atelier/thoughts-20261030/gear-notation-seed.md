# Gear-shift notation (the `*` marks): a reader's guide, drafted

His messages carry a small set of marks. They are not typos or noise. Each one tells a reader how to take the words around it. This page is a first guess at what they mean, written so that any navigo reading his messages can follow them. **He is the only one who can correct it.**

## The marks, as he used them

| Mark | His words about it | My reading |
|---|---|---|
| `*` | "* = interrupt" | An interrupt: a thought that cut in. Take what follows as an aside, then go back to the thread that was running. |
| `*¿` | "interupt - short term flush into background. i can recall this without re-reading. I was ready for it." | An interrupt that is parked at once. He keeps it in mind without rereading it. A reader should not make a big reply to it, and should not lose it. |
| `*gear shift` | after "one message setup, one message prep, one message create" | A change of mode or gait. What follows is run differently from what came before. |
| `*reset to origin` | (the mark alone) | Go back to the starting point of the thought, before the detours. |
| `clutch in` / `clutch out` with a time (12:04, 13:51, 14:07) | "clutch in 14:07 (going to find the lexeme so I spell it correctly)" | `clutch in`: step out of the flow to do something (look a word up, check, get ready). `clutch out`: back in gear. The time lets a later reader see how long the pause was. |
| `quiet cruise control` | after `clutch out 13:51` | A steady stretch with no interrupts. |
| `* (no time mark)` | "add in * (no time mark)" | An interrupt placed on purpose with no clock time. |
| Bare times and numbers between lines | a running log | Personal log lines. Left out of the repo on purpose (see the README). |

## Why it matters now

- He says the Latin lexeme is "working way beyond expectations", and that his spelling is critical for it, so he will interrupt more, "until it settles into my normal language". Expect more `*` and `clutch` marks while he checks spellings. A `clutch in` for a spelling is not a change of subject.
- He wants the lexeme in all his backgrounds, a terminal always at hand. The plain list already exists as `docs/latin-lexemes.txt`.
- He asks for a "structured Novissimum" built "step by step, turn by turn, or moment by moment". *Novissimum* is the rear guard: closing loose ends. A pattern for closing a conversation could use these marks the same way.

## A reader's rules (proposal)

1. Do not "fix" a mark or a spelling in his words. Keep them as written when quoting.
2. After a `*`, finish the aside and return to the thread that was running.
3. After a `*¿`, keep it, answer it lightly or not at all, and do not make him reread it.
4. After `*reset to origin`, drop the detours and go back to the first thing.
5. When unsure what a mark meant, say how you are reading it before acting (the same rule the Latin calls use).

## Not known

- Whether `clutch in` always means "pause to look something up", or sometimes "start" (it opens the hat's speech at 12:04).
- What exactly `*gear shift` changes: the mode of the work, the pace, or the voice.
- Whether these should be written into the Latin workflow documents, or stay his private shorthand.
