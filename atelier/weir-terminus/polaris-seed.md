## 1. Many witnesses, one sky

Someone lies on the ground and looks up through the eye's own small lens. A camera,
held to the same sky, catches its own version. Animals come by and stand near.
Small creatures on the ground and in the trees look up at the same stars. Plants and
soil answer to light and to dark without anything we would call an eye.

So the camera is one witness among many. It is not *the* witness. What it caught
was Polaris, and that is all that is claimed.

This carries one quiet question: *what is traditional vision?* Sensing light is
older and wider than eyes. The Terminus takes it as a standing reminder that a record
made by a device is one way of witnessing, next to others.

**Which picture is most like an ocular?** Two pictures sit in `sky/`. The photo is
light as a camera caught it, and it is the nearer thing to a view through an eye.
The chart is the same sky as named points and relations. An AI reader has no ocular:
it receives both as data. Of the two, the chart is nearer to how an AI holds a sky,
and the photo is nearer to what the sky is like to stand under. Both are kept.

## 2. Coming from Polaris

Turn the picture around. Looking *toward* Polaris, it is the fixed point. Coming
*from* it, the sky is unrecognizable:

- Polaris is roughly 430 to 450 light-years away, so the light the camera caught
  left about four centuries ago.
- The Big Dipper is a viewing angle. Its stars lie at different distances, roughly
  80 to 125 light-years, and from Polaris there is no Dipper.
- The Sun would be far too faint to see with the eye.
- Polaris itself is not steady: it varies slightly in brightness over about four days.
  The fixed point breathes.
- It is the pole star for now. Over about 26,000 years the pole drifts to other stars.

**What it does to the Terminus.** In the cave picture the starlight already falls
*from* Polaris onto the crystal and down into the pool. Read from the star's side,
the water and light start at the fixed point and travel down through the wells, and
the Terminus is where the journey ends. Dubhe and Merak point at Polaris from here.
From there they would point home.
