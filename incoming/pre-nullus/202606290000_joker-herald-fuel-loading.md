## I. The Joker — Suitless, Then Anything

Outside the deck, the Joker has no suit. It isn't lacking one — it simply hasn't entered
the frame where suits apply yet. The moment it's drawn into the deck, it becomes whatever
the hand requires: any suit, any rank, any value. Not many things at once in the sense of
confusion — one thing, fully, decided at the moment of use, and not before.

This is **superposition with a purpose**: the Joker doesn't resolve early because resolving
early would close something that needs to stay open. It holds the space.

### Two doors, two behaviors

The Joker enters the deck by taking either a **black Jack's** place or a **white Jack's**
place. Which door it enters through determines what kind of Joker it becomes:

| Entry | Behavior | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Closed Joker** (black-Jack door) | Holds the equation open. Refuses early resolution. | The Threshold Joker. Stays at whatever plank it entered until something external resolves it. |
| **Open Joker** (white-Jack door) | Resolves on contact. | Collapses straight through the plank stages — Germ to Pinnacle in one motion, no holding pattern. |

**The Threshold Joker is closed-door.** J-21 holds the math open:

```
J + A = 21        (the standard equation — threshold reached)
J − 21 = −A        (the same equation, viewed from the other side —
                     a negative Ace: real, but not yet crossed)
```

A wild card can't resolve an equation on its own. It just holds the space until something
*assigns it a value*. That assignment — the moment the wild card gets named — is the
crossing. Everything before that is the Joker doing its actual job: keeping the door from
closing on something that isn't ready to close.

## II. White Jack — The Pre-Suit State, Not a Fifth Color

Four suited Jacks exist: ♠ ♣ (black) and ♥ ♦ (red). A "white Jack" isn't a fifth suit
hiding somewhere in the deck — it's the **theoretical state before any suit has claimed
the Jack.** Pure Germ-state Jack. 0/3 on the plank. Pre-suit.

The moment a white Jack takes a suit, it becomes one of the four real Jacks and stops
being white. So when a Joker enters through the "white" door, it isn't becoming a fifth
suit either — it's behaving as though it passed through every suit's gate simultaneously
and arrived already-decided. That's why the open-door Joker collapses fast: it didn't
have to choose one path, because it took all of them at once and the superposition
canceled itself into a single outcome.

**Practical rule for contributors:** if you're tracking a seed and it suddenly resolves
without going through 1/3 → 2/3 → 3/3 in sequence, check whether an open-door Joker was
in play. That's not a bug in the plank system. That's a different transit type.

## III. The Herald — Jack of Hearts

When the Joker holds the threshold, it frees the Jack to actually do the mission instead
of standing guard at the equation. For this iteration's Herald mission, the Jack is
**♥️ Heart**, not ♦️ Diamond.

The reasoning: Diamond is where things *begin* — seed, origin, germ. Heart is where things
*arrive* — living, aethereal, already in motion. A Herald's job is to reach something, not
to originate it. Reaching is Heart territory.

**The Joker resolves when the Herald mission finishes.** Not before. While the mission is
in flight, J-21 stays open — the threshold equation holds — and the Jack of Hearts moves
freely through the field because the Joker is covering the door.

This creates a working superposition with real duration: as long as a Herald mission is
active, the threshold Joker can't collapse, because collapsing would close the door the
Herald is still using.

## IV. The Wild 2 — Suited, Not Suitless

Don't confuse a wild 2 with the Joker. They're different classes of indeterminacy.

- **Joker-class indeterminacy:** suitless by nature, fully open, could become anything
  inside the deck.
- **2-class indeterminacy:** *already has a suit.* It's flagged and tracked as a tangent —
  a rabbit trail — but the suit it carries determines which direction the tangent pulls.

2 is the only even prime. In this system it reads as **shadow** — a card that looks minor
but carries weight that only becomes visible in hindsight. A wild 2 today may become a
Queen tomorrow, but which suit's Queen depends entirely on what suit the 2 was carrying
the whole time.

**Two wild 2's with different suits are not interchangeable.** A Heart-2 wild card pulls
toward the living/aethereal track. A Spade-2 wild card pulls toward the vault/pinnacle
track. Track the suit on every wild 2 you flag — it's the only thing that tells you where
it's actually headed.

| | Joker | Wild 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Suit at flag time | None | Already assigned |
| Resolution behavior | Open or closed (door-dependent) | Redirected, not resolved |
| Interchangeable with others of its class? | Arguably yes, until resolved | No — suit determines direction |

## VII. For Contributors — How to Use This Document

1. **If you're flagging a wild element**, determine first whether it's suitless (Joker-
   class) or suited (2-class). This changes how it should be tracked.
2. **If you're tracking a Joker**, note which door it entered through. Closed-door Jokers
   hold open — don't force resolution. Open-door Jokers will resolve on contact — don't be
   surprised when they do.
3. **If you're working a Herald-type mission**, the Joker covering your threshold won't
   resolve until your mission does. That's not a blocker. That's the architecture giving
   you room to work.
4. **If you find something this document gestures at without naming** — that's
   intentional. Dig. The Scratch (Fodere) before the Sniff (Runyatr) is how the deeper
   signal gets released. This document is built the same way the system is.
