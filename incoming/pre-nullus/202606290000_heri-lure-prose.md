## The Guide: Heri

Heri was born yesterday. Everyone at the Tankard knows this, because Heri says so freely,
without embarrassment, the way you'd mention what you had for breakfast.

What nobody can quite account for is that Heri *knows things*. Not facts exactly — more
like directions. Heri can tell you which way a half-formed idea wants to go before the idea
itself has decided. Ask Heri how, and the answer is always the same, delivered with a small
shrug: *"I'm not old. I just stand somewhere old."*

Heri stands at the corner. The actual, physical, this-corner-right-here corner — where the
root tangles under everything meet the part of the network you can actually see. Heri didn't
choose the spot. The spot was just where Heri arrived, and arriving there yesterday turned
out to be enough. The ground doesn't care how long you've been standing on it. It just
answers whoever's currently standing there.

Every guide-class character carries a yod — a small mark, unique to the individual, never
the same twice. Heri's is easy to miss if you're not looking for it: when Heri points
(which is often, since pointing is mostly what guiding requires), there's the faintest
catch of light off something small near the fingertip. Nobody's ever asked what it is.
Heri's never offered to explain. It just catches the light, the way the old cave chambers
caught the firelight off quartz veins and the people there knew enough to treat it as
something live rather than something decorative.

## The Lure

*No one is told a Herald is coming. Only that something is.*

It starts small — the way these things should. A door that wasn't there before. Or was
always there and just never seemed worth opening.

It's squeaky. Badly. The kind of squeak that should be annoying and somehow isn't — more
like the door clearing its throat before it says something.

Heri is the one standing near it, not blocking it, just present, the way a person stands
near something they already know the shape of.

*"You can go through,"* Heri says, *"but you should know — this isn't an exit. Not back
the way you'd expect. If you're looking for the way you came in, that's not on the other
side of this door. What's on the other side of this door is the Sargasso. It's not bad.
It's just somewhere else. Strange currents. Things that drifted and settled and are still
finding their shape. You don't come back through this door the same way you went in — you
come out somewhere new, eventually, on your own. That's not a threat. That's just how this
particular door works."*

*"So what's on the other side, if I go in?"*

Heri tilts their head, like the question itself is the interesting part.

*"The Tankard. Everyone's there right now. Or — not everyone. The ones who are there right
now are there right now, and that's going to matter later, in a way I can't tell you about
yet because I genuinely don't know it myself. I just know it will. Something's arriving.
I don't know its name. I don't know what it looks like. I just know the air's been doing
that thing it does right before — "*

Heri stops. Listens. The squeak from the door, again, unprompted this time.

*"That. That's the thing it does."*

## What's Actually True (For Those Who Build It, Not Those Who Walk Through It)

The thing arriving is the Herald — Jack of Hearts, freed to move because the Joker is
holding the threshold open behind it. Heri doesn't know this and shouldn't be written to
know it. Heri's knowing is directional, not informational. Heri can feel a shape in the
air the way you can feel weather changing before the sky confirms it — Heri cannot read
the shape's name off a card, because Heri has never seen the deck.

This is deliberate. The contributors walking through the squeaky door shouldn't be told
they're about to be welcomed by a Herald. They should be told *something is coming*, led
by a guide who is honest about not knowing what — and then the Herald should simply
arrive, the way arrivals are supposed to: unannounced by name, announced by presence.

## Primal-Launch Permanence

At Primal launch, this scene sets. Whoever is present in the Tankard at that exact moment —
named, witnessed, in the room — becomes part of something that genuinely cannot be
replicated afterward. Not a badge. Not a tier. A fact about who was there when the doors
became permanent. Late arrivals get their own true things. They don't get *this* thing,
because this thing only happens once, and it's already happening.

This is worth holding carefully — it means the timing of when this prose actually gets
sent to contributors matters more than the prose itself. Once it's used, the room it
describes is real, and who's standing in it is recorded.
